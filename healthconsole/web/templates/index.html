Summary

How Summary works

Each card is the headline of one view, read with your own access for the scope in use. Your persona picks the cards and their order (personas: → summary); Settings → Summary sets how many items each lists and the thresholds below.

  • Status line: one colour per card. Problems: red with a critical problem, yellow with a warning. Security: red when a resource reachable from the internet has high vulnerabilities, yellow with any warning or high finding. Cost: red with a budget spent, yellow with a spike or a budget past the warning share (or forecast past 100%). Readiness: red or yellow by its worst finding. Copy summary puts it on the first line.
  • Cost comes from your cost store (see Cost → How this works): this month, where it's heading at its rate so far, the last 30 days, and budgets (one read per subscription, kept an hour). A 7-day change that includes the 1st of the month says so: monthly charges are booked that day.
  • Security is Security's exposure and configuration checks plus Defender's vulnerability totals.
  • Readiness shows the last full report for this scope, or runs its quick checks (no Kubernetes or Key Vault reads) for the peak date last used on Readiness.
  • What changed compares with the summary from at least a day ago (Settings → Summary). Each summary is kept on this machine, readable only by you, for a week by default; Clear cached data removes them.

Export
How this works

Costs are kept on this machine and updated every 6 hours; Refresh gets the latest now (a big scope takes a few minutes). Actual cost (as invoiced) or Amortized (reservations and savings plans spread over their days and charged to what uses them; read and kept apart, the first time you pick it). This month is compared with the same days of last month, and purchases (a reservation's monthly payment, billed on one day) are counted in This month but kept out of the 7-day figures and spikes. From Azure Cost Management, read with your own access and kept on this machine (cache_dir, readable only by you), so subscriptions, resource groups, Summary and this view are answered from what's kept instead of asking Azure each time. Azure throttles cost queries to about two dozen subscriptions a minute, so the first read of every subscription you can see runs in the background: the scope in use first, the last 30 days of each before the rest. After that, a subscription older than 6 hours has only its last 3 days read again (older days don't change). Refresh rereads the scope now; Clear cached data in your account menu forgets everything kept.

How this works

The people (not apps or managed identities) who can do more than read without PIM in the scope's subscriptions: role assignments that are standing, or made through PIM as active with no end, assigned to the person or to a group they're in. A PIM activation, which ends by itself, doesn't count. One read of assignments and one of PIM-eligible roles per subscription, plus Microsoft Graph for group members. Read-only, with your own access. The last result per scope is kept on this machine, readable only by you, for audit.keep_days (7 by default) and opens at once with its age; Refresh reads it again. Clear cached data removes it.

Pre-peak readiness

Before a busy period (a sale, a launch, a seasonal peak): if traffic doubled, what would stop the scope above from scaling, and what would expire before the busy period is over? One report, read-only.

How Readiness works

Peak ends on is the last day of your busy period. It's only used for expiry: a certificate (or, if ticked, a secret or key) that expires before that date (or has already expired) is red, one that expires within the warning days after it (Settings → expiry warning days, 30 by default) is yellow. Everything else is checked as it stands now: how much room is left to grow.

  • Scaling headroom: each autoscale setting, AKS node pool and App Service plan against its maximum. At the maximum is red; at 80% or more is yellow, because it can't absorb double the load.
  • vCPU quota: free vCPUs per subscription, region and VM family, against what the pools and scale sets in scope would add at their maximum. Quota is shared with everything else in the subscription.
  • Subnet IPs: subnets running out of addresses, and whether each AKS subnet can hold its pools at their maximum.
  • Single points of failure: single-instance plans and pools and, with the Kubernetes checks, one-replica workloads and PodDisruptionBudgets that block node drains.
  • HPAs: each HorizontalPodAutoscaler's replicas against its maximum, and ones that can't scale.
  • Certificates: expiry against the peak end date, read from Key Vaults and App Service.

The basic checks are a few Resource Graph and quota reads. Kubernetes and Key Vault checks add one request per cluster and per vault, so they take longer on a big scope. Each item says what to do before the peak.

    Integrations

    Connections to ServiceNow and monitoring tools for the tenant you're signed in to: each tenant has its own, and switching tenant switches them. Read-only: the console only reads from them.

    Alerts

    Keep the page open (or just the console running) and be told when something stops being normal. The console checks each rule on its interval and alerts on a change only: a status that gets worse or something new, never something that was already red at the first check, and an alert resolves itself once it is back to how it was. Alerts stay on this machine: nothing is sent anywhere.

    Logs

    What happened in this console, on this machine: errors, what was done here, and every request it sent to Azure and the tools it reads from. Use it to find an error ID the page showed, or to see that only reads left.

    Azure Health Console

    One place to check the health of everything an app depends on, fast, from your laptop: during an incident, or before a peak. It answers "this resource and everything connected to it: is any of it unhealthy, and what changed?" from what Azure reports, next to ServiceNow and your monitoring tool. It doesn't replace your APM; it answers a different question.

    How it works

    • Your access, nobody else's. It reads with your own Azure CLI sign-in (and your PIM roles). It has no access of its own, so it shows exactly what you're allowed to see.
    • Read-only by construction. Every request to Azure, Kubernetes, ServiceNow and monitoring tools goes through a guard that refuses anything that isn't a read, before it's sent.
    • Stays on your machine. It runs on 127.0.0.1, keeps settings in your config folder and secrets in the operating system's keychain, and sends nothing about you anywhere else. Its only other reads are public pages: Azure's status feed, and PyPI: whether a newer version is out (every 6 hours, updates.check) and the numbers below when About opens.
    • Works in any tenant. No organisation's names or conventions are built in: tags, scopes and rules come from what it finds.
